# RULER Results

Legend for dataset abbreviations used in the per-dataset tables below:
`ns1=niah_single_1, ns2=niah_single_2, ns3=niah_single_3, nmk1=niah_multikey_1, nmk2=niah_multikey_2, nmk3=niah_multikey_3, nmq=niah_multiquery, nmv=niah_multivalue, cwe=cwe, fwe=fwe, vt=vt`

All scores are `string_match_all` on the full 500-sample test set per subtask (mean over 11 subtasks = the "Uniform"/"Winner"/etc. summary score).

"Winner shape" = unconstrained-NAS winner's shape rescaled to the target budget. "Best random" = best of the LHS-random init points in the budget-constrained (Step-4) search. "Best BO" = best point actually proposed by the BO-guided phase of the budget-constrained search (can be worse than random/winner — see notes).

**Important methodology asymmetry between the two methods' 2nd NAS (Step-4):** for **H2O**, which config counted as "Winner shape" / "Best random" / "Best BO" was decided by **calibration-subset scores** (the 10% NAS-fitness sample) during the search — the config picked as best-on-calibration was then evaluated once on the full 500-sample test set to get the numbers in this file. For **SnapKV**, the same selection was instead made by **test scores** — i.e., the candidates were picked/confirmed using full-test evaluation, not the calibration subsample. This means the two methods' "Best BO"/"Best random" columns are not selected on a like-for-like basis, and any SnapKV vs. H2O comparison of these columns should account for that.

**Second methodology asymmetry — what "Best BO" actually means differs by method.** For **AdaKV**, "Best BO" is the isolated best of *only* the true BO-guided search rows (rows 64+ of `output.txt`, i.e. after the 7 shape anchors + 57 LHS random points) — see its Best Heuristic/Best Random/Best BO columns in the per-dataset tables below, which separate all five categories (uniform/heuristic/winner/random/BO) explicitly. For **SnapKV and H2O**, "Best BO" in the summary tables below is actually `best_overall` — the single best score across *all* rows of the search (uniform + heuristics + winner + random + BO combined), produced by the automated `watch_slices_and_eval.sh` pipeline, which does not isolate the BO phase. So despite the column header, SnapKV/H2O's "Best BO" value is **not guaranteed to come from the BO phase at all** — it could equally be the winner-seed row, a random LHS point, or a plain heuristic shape, whichever happened to score best overall.

This was checked directly against the raw `output.txt` files (`NAS_Assets/select_5way_snapshot.py`, which isolates `argmin(f2[rows 64+])` as the true BO-only best) for every SnapKV/H2O RULER Step-4 run that exists:

| Method | Budget | `best_overall`'s true region | Genuinely BO? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SnapKV | 1024 | BO | ✅ yes |
| SnapKV | 1536 | **winner** (row 6) | ❌ no — the "Best BO" value (98.35) is actually the winner shape's own score |
| SnapKV | 2048 | **winner** (row 6) | ❌ no — the true isolated best-BO score (verified separately: 98.12) is actually *worse* than the winner shape (98.91) |
| H2O | 1024 | BO | ✅ yes |
| H2O | 1536 | BO | ✅ yes |
| H2O | 2048 | BO | ✅ yes |

The two SnapKV mismatches were already caught and correctly reflected in the SnapKV summary table's "Best config" column ("Winner (BO converged to same config)" / "Winner (BO's own best was actually worse)") — but the **"Best BO" column value itself is still the non-BO `best_overall` score** in those two rows, not a genuine BO-phase result, so it should not be read as "the BO algorithm found 98.35/98.12." All three of H2O's "Best BO" values check out as genuinely BO-sourced. No SnapKV/H2O RULER Step-4 search was ever run at 64/128/256/512 (confirmed: no `output.txt` exists for those budgets — see the "only config evaluated" rows above), so this check covers every Step-4 run that exists for these two methods.

**Open caveat — AdaKV was computed on a different server.** SnapKV and H2O's numbers in this file come from SNAP_NAS. AdaKV's benchmark/search/eval were run on **LLM WS** instead — `Status.md` documents a known score discrepancy for AdaKV specifically between these two environments, attributed to a torch-version difference, that has **not been resolved or quantified**. Treat AdaKV's absolute scores, and any AdaKV-vs-SnapKV/H2O comparison, with that caveat until AdaKV's final configs are re-scored on SNAP_NAS (or the gap between environments is measured directly). AdaKV's Step-4 selection basis (calibration-subset vs. test score, the same axis documented above for H2O/SnapKV) is also not yet confirmed — do not assume it matches either of the other two.

**Why Winner shape roughly ties (even slightly loses to) Uniform at 128/256:** these four low budgets (64/128/256/512) never had a Step-4 (budget-constrained) search run — the "Winner shape" here is just H2O's **Step-1 native winner** (found and selected via calibration score at its own native budget ≈2180, where H2O already scores 44-63) rescaled down to the target budget. This is a different mechanism than the Step-4 calibration-selection caveat noted above — it isn't that this particular winner was cherry-picked unfairly; it's that a shape optimized to look good at a ~2180-budget, mid-performance regime doesn't automatically transfer to a near-floor 128/256-budget regime. Concretely, at B128 the winner gains on `nmk1`/`nmk2`/`fwe` but loses on `cwe` (22.08 vs 30.28) and `vt` (2.92 vs 6.44) relative to uniform — the two roughly cancel out (net -0.11). Given the swings are small (≤0.4 pts) and flip sign between 64/512 (positive) and 128/256 (negative), this reads as a wash / within-noise result at these specific budgets rather than a systematic failure — and it's a natural argument for why running Step-4 at 64-512 too (not yet done) would be worthwhile.

Winner config, non-rescaled: avg_budget = **2180** (from `anchors/anchor_h2o_2180.txt`) — score is N/A, this specific config was never evaluated as a standalone row locally (only via rescaling to fixed budgets).

- At 1024/1536/2048, H2O's BO-guided search meaningfully beats its own winner-rescaled shape (+9 to +16 pts), whereas SnapKV's winner-rescaled shape is essentially never beaten by the constrained search (ties at 1536, loses at 2048).

### Why H2O's Winner-vs-Uniform gap grows with budget (+0.37 → +29.69) while SnapKV's doesn't (+10.35 → +5.98, non-monotonic)

Per-dataset numbers point to a specific cause: **H2O's uniform baseline is structurally unable to solve `niah_single_3` at any budget up to 2048** — it scores exactly 0.00 on that subtask at every one of the 7 tested budgets, regardless of how much total budget is given, because spreading budget evenly across all 32 layers never gives the one or two layers that matter for that needle enough tokens to retain it. The Winner shape is *also* stuck at 0.00 on `niah_single_3` through budget 1536, but at 2048 it suddenly jumps to **80.40** — that single subtask's jump alone contributes roughly +7.3 points to the overall +29.69 mean gap at B2048. `niah_multikey_3` shows the same pattern less extremely (Uniform reaches only 48.60 by 2048 vs Winner's 87.20). Since H2O's Best-BO configs unlock `niah_single_3` even earlier (63.00 already at B1024, 99.40 at B2048 — see per-dataset tables), this is really the mechanism the method is designed to exploit: H2O's default eviction policy leaves a *specific, fixable* hole that only non-uniform layer allocation can close, and that hole widens in absolute score-points as more budget becomes available for the "correct" layers to exploit.

SnapKV's uniform baseline, by contrast, is not structurally stuck on the same subtasks — its own `niah_single_3` score climbs from 0.0 (B64) to 92.8 (B2048) *under uniform allocation alone*, and `niah_multikey_3` reaches 43.0 by B2048 unaided. So there's much less low-hanging fruit left for the Winner shape to capture at high budget, which is why SnapKV's Winner-vs-Uniform gap stays modest and doesn't grow monotonically — the baseline is simply less brittle to begin with.

**Takeaway for the paper**: the size of the layer-wise-allocation benefit tracks how much headroom the underlying eviction policy's uniform baseline leaves on the table. H2O leaves much more on the table (weaker/brittler uniform baseline, specific subtasks it cannot solve unaided), so the method's payoff for H2O grows dramatically with budget; SnapKV's uniform baseline is comparatively strong on its own, so the method still helps everywhere but with smaller, non-monotonic margins. This is a reasonable and fairly compelling story to tell explicitly rather than something to explain away.

### Observations

- **Layer-importance patterns differ meaningfully between methods.** SnapKV's high-budget layers are spread across mid-to-late layers (5, 10, 14-17, 20, 22-24). H2O's high-budget layers are heavily front-loaded (0, 1, 2, 7, 8, 10-17) with a couple of late-layer additions (23, 24, 27). This is consistent with — and further evidence for — the earlier finding that layer-importance is method-specific, not just model-specific: SnapKV and H2O evict tokens by different criteria, so the layers that most need extra budget to compensate differ too.
- **SnapKV's BO@2048 config substantially overlaps with (but doesn't exactly reproduce) its native winner's high-budget layers**: 8 of the native winner's 10 max-budget layers (5, 10, 15, 16, 17, 22, 23, 24) are also max-budget in the BO config; layers 14 and 20 drop out, and BO adds 8, 11, 21, 25 instead. This near-but-not-exact match is consistent with BO@2048 scoring *slightly worse* than the winner shape (98.12 vs 98.91) — a close variant, not an improvement.
- **H2O's BO@2048 config diverges more substantially from its native winner**: only 9 of the native winner's 16 max-budget layers (1, 7, 8, 10, 14, 15, 16, 23, 24) survive as max-budget in the BO config; layers 0, 2, 11, 12, 13, 17, 27 drop out of the high set, while 4, 5, 9, 26 are newly promoted to max-budget. Unlike SnapKV, this is a meaningfully different allocation, not a minor perturbation — and it scores clearly *better* than the transferred winner (96.14 vs 92.51), reinforcing that H2O's constrained search found a genuinely different, more effective shape rather than just rediscovering the unconstrained winner.
